# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Changed (classification)
- The classification by homology uses the HMM classifier (`scripts/pantera_classify.R`, libraries `pantera_hmm_v1`) instead of blastp against RepeatMasker's peptide library and the XGBoost model. Each consensus gets one call from protein domain HMMs, TIR end models and end partners, Helitron end models or SINE models, with a confidence (`high`, `low`, `weak`, `none`). The call's label becomes the class through `model/label_map.tsv` (RepeatMasker names, refined by the sub label when RepeatMasker has a name for it, e.g. `DNA/TcMar-Pogo`, `DNA/hAT-hobo`); without a call the class is `Unknown`. A protein sub label is not reported when it is the only lineage of its label that scored on the element (`class_cfg$min_sub_models`; with a single lineage modelled, every CR1 would be Zenon), except EnSpm under DNA/CMC (`class_cfg$single_sub_ok`): every EnSpm call fell on an EnSpm/CACTA element and none on Chapaev, so CACTA is `DNA/CMC-EnSpm`. The structure rules are unchanged.
- Stats file: the columns `homology_class` and `homology_prob` are replaced by `homology_label`, `homology_confidence`, `homology_evidence`, `homology_sub`, `homology_domains`, `homology_alternatives`, `homology_flags` and `homology_notes` (README, "Classification").
- Results, with the same alignments. Test data: 6 of 17 elements change class, none changes `pass`: 5 elements called `DNA` by their TIRs now have a superfamily (CMC-Transib, P, TcMar-Pogo, TcMar-Tc1, hAT-hobo), and one hAT becomes hAT-hobo. Five D. melanogaster genomes (168 elements, compared with RepBase 31.10): 31 change class and 3 change `pass`. Gains: 6 `DNA` elements get their superfamily (P, MULE-MuDR); 8 of the 10 elements called TcMar-Tc1 (the other 2 stay TcMar-Tc1) and 1 called `DNA` are TcMar-Pogo (RepBase POGO, POGON1), and 3 hAT are hAT-hobo; 7 Jockey fragments that were Unknown are LINE/I-Jockey (still rejected). Losses: 3 Jockey consensi with ORF1 only and 1 shorter one get no call, because no HMM covers LINE ORF1: 2 that were accepted as LINE are rejected, and one is accepted as "Unknown: large cluster". 2 BURDOCK consensi (GAG hit below threshold) stay accepted, as `LTR` instead of `LTR/Gypsy`. Accepted elements with the right class: 94 of 96 (was 96 of 97); right subclass: 92 of 96 (was 90 of 97). Classification took about as long as before (171 s against 152 s for 195 consensi, 10 threads).
- No longer used: getorf (EMBOSS), the R package xgboost, `libs/RepeatPeps.lib*` and `model/{typesnames,featurenames,xgbmodel.ubj}`. New tools needed: HMMER, miniprot, Infernal, TRF (and DIAMOND for the optional host-protein part). The recipe and the repository files change together with the Zenodo record of `pantera_hmm_v1` (next patch).
- `-d` also keeps the classifier's per-chunk files (`hmm_classifier/`).
- `scripts/run_dev.sh` uses the clone's `model/hmm/` when it is there.

### Changed (a homology call is enough)
- Elements of any class (not Unknown) with a high or low homology call that no class structure rule accepts are accepted on the call (`reason` "<class>: <evidence> call", e.g. "LINE: protein call"). The structure rules (a long ORF and a tail for LINEs, terminal LTRs over 100 bp, TIRs at the ends, SINEs under 500 bp) now decide only for weak calls and Unknowns; the rejection rules after them (LTR structure mostly tandem repeat, solo LTRs and internal deletions, too many Ns, microsatellites, LINEs mostly a repeat of themselves, `-e` copies in one genome, contained elements) still apply. This replaces the rule of patch 8, which did the same for DNA elements with a superfamily call only. The log gives the number of elements accepted this way.
- Results, same masked alignments, 14 genome sets (accepted elements): 2128 -> 2155. 29 elements accepted on their call: 17 LINEs with ORFs of 381-1473 nt (Biomphalaria glabrata 9, Nematostella vectensis 4, Ephydatia muelleri 2, E. cirrhosa 2; among them RTE_19-Bglab, whose 3' end Unknown_79 had been accepted as an Unknown and is now contained in it), 5 Ngaro elements (tested as LTR elements, though DIRS-group elements have no LTRs), 5 Gypsy elements with short LTRs (84-90 bp) or LTRs away from the ends, a 639 bp tRNA SINE and a class-only DNA element with TIRs 8 bp from the ends. 3 more such elements (RTE_37-Bglab, Ngaro_5-Oedul, Ngaro_6-Oedul) are now contained in newly accepted ones and stay rejected, and 2 accepted elements become contained (Unknown_79-Bglab, Ngaro-NA_1-Oedul). D. melanogaster and HG002: no change (the 11 D. melanogaster elements with one copy per strain stay rejected by `-e`). Test data: unchanged.

### Changed (Unknowns with a long ORF)
- Unknowns that no other rule accepts or rejects are accepted when their longest ORF is at least `rules_cfg$unknown_min_orf` (900 nt, 300 aa) and a TSD supports their edges in at least `rules_cfg$unknown_orf_tsd_conf` (0.5) of the copies, the target not in a short tandem repeat ("Unknown: long ORF and TSD"; the log gives their number). Rejection rules that come later (too many Ns, microsatellite, too few copies, contained) still apply. The stats columns `orf1`-`orf3` are in nucleotides (README).
- Results, same masked alignments as patch 9: 11 elements accepted, nothing else changes. Schmidtea mediterranea 126 -> 134 (8 Unknowns of 2.4-4.1 kb with 0.9-2.3 kb ORFs), Biomphalaria glabrata 509 -> 511 (two Unknowns of 8.3-8.4 kb with 2.8 kb ORFs), E. cirrhosa 116 -> 117 (one of 2.8 kb). The other sets have no such element. Test data unchanged.

### Changed (orthologous copies, Unknown rule)
- Orthologous copies count once. Copies from different genomes whose flanks next to the insertion (60 bp each side, `locus_cfg`) share 16-mers on both sides, in either orientation, are one insertion found in several genomes (one locus; `copy_loci`, one join on canonical k-mers per loop 2 cluster). When a loop 2 cluster has at least `-m` loci, one copy per locus (the longest) is aligned; with fewer loci all copies are aligned, as before. Orthologous copies have conserved flanks, which failed the segmental-duplication test (`loop2_cfg$min_trimmed`) and made the TSD look better supported than it is: in five D. melanogaster strains, 5 of the 16 copies of the I element were one insertion, its cluster had 136 columns outside the conserved block (180 needed) and gave no consensus; one copy per locus gives 321.
- Stats file: new column `loci` (after `cluster_size`, which still counts copies): the number of loci of the element's cluster.
- Unknown elements are accepted when found at more than `rules_cfg$unknown_min_loci` (5) loci ("Unknown: many loci"), instead of a cluster larger than a kneedle cut-off on the cluster sizes ("Unknown: large cluster"). The cut-off moved between runs with nearly the same cluster sizes (Ostrea edulis 12 and 5, Schmidtea mediterranea 5 and 11), and grew with the number of genomes, since one polymorphic insertion seen in many genomes made a large cluster. The `kneedle` function and the R package quantmod (used only by it) are no longer needed.
- Speed: the segment flank table (`seg_flanks`) is keyed once after reading, so that its per-cluster lookups (`seg_flank_lengths`, `seg_overlaps`) no longer sort it each time (a 50-copy lookup took 64 ms, now 20 ms with the locus search included). `copy_loci` takes about 20 ms for 50 copies and 0.35 s for 1000. D. melanogaster, 6 threads, same alignments: loop 2 653 s -> 618 s (mafft 3757 -> 3346 s, fewer sequences); the whole run 1491 s -> 1732 s, because 9 more consensi are classified and measured.
- Results, same masked alignments as fc69b29 (accepted elements): 2068 -> 2117 over 14 genome sets; 2052 of the 2068 are found again (one element covering at least 80% at 80% identity), 2034 of them by an accepted element. D. melanogaster 101 -> 107: the I element (IVK_DM), R1 (DMRT1A) and INVADER2 are back, RepBase families matched by accepted elements 48 -> 51, none lost. HG002 14 -> 13, Dfam families matched by any element 17 -> 19 (AluYh3, LTR13, LTR5 and SVA_D gained, MER11C and SVA_B lost), two L1 3' ends accepted as Unknowns are now classified L1 and rejected as contained. Biomphalaria glabrata 481 -> 509 (25 Unknowns of 6-15 loci, the kneedle cut-off was 15), Ephydatia muelleri 302 -> 317, Ostrea edulis 576 -> 582, Schmidtea mediterranea 132 -> 126 (Unknowns of at most 5 loci, and 3 LTRs whose TSD or ends, now from independent copies, fail the TG..CA rule), Pristionchus pacificus 34 -> 31, other sets within 2. Test data: library identical, only the new column.

### Changed (DNA transposons, non-autonomous elements, tandem masking by default)
- Tandem masking is the default: the tandem arrays of each genome at least `-w` bp long are soft masked before aligning, as `-t` did. `-N/--no_tandem_mask` turns it off; `-t` is still accepted and does nothing; `-t` with `-N` stops with an error. Masking against no masking (1c83fb9, 11 new genome pairs, D. melanogaster, HG002, E. cirrhosa): accepted elements 1969 -> 1986; 1945 of the 1969 found again (one element of the other run covering at least 80% at 80% identity), 1930 of them by an accepted element. The tandem arrays masked do not overlap the elements lost (checked for Ephydatia Gypsy and the D. melanogaster I element): their copies align as without masking, and the differences come from the clustering of a different set of segments. Most `pass` changes come from the Unknown cut-off (kneedle on the cluster sizes), which moves between runs with nearly the same cluster sizes (Ostrea edulis 12 -> 5, Nematostella vectensis 9 -> 5, Biomphalaria glabrata 12 -> 15). D. melanogaster: 101 -> 101, RepBase benchmark unchanged, but the I element is lost: its loop 2 cluster gets no consensus because 5 of its 16 copies are the same insertion in the five strains, whose flanks are conserved (segmental duplication test, 136 columns outside the conserved block, 180 needed).
- DNA elements with a superfamily call from the classifier (protein, TIR end models or end partner; confidence `high` or `low`) are accepted without the structural TIR test (`reason` "DNA: <evidence> call"). The test needs a TIR within 8 bp of both ends (`rules_cfg$end_gap`); in 11 new genome pairs it rejected 74 such elements (no repeat found 30, a direct repeat instead 18, a TIR 8 bp or more from an end 26), among them PiggyBac, Kolobok and hAT elements with high-confidence transposase calls.
- Non-autonomous elements found by shared termini with low termini identity (`partner_type` `NA_low`) are accepted (`reason` "non-autonomous (low termini identity): termini of <partner>"). They kept their `-NA` class but were rejected unless another rule accepted them.
- Elements whose ends are those of a known DNA transposon but whose call is another class: when the classifier's TIR end models or end partner match both ends with a DNA superfamily (new stats column `homology_ends`) and the element is called another class (e.g. a LINE on a reverse transcriptase), the ends decide. The element is a non-autonomous DNA transposon carrying another TE's protein: class `DNA/<superfamily>-NA`, accepted, `reason` "non-autonomous DNA: <ends> ends, <class> protein inside". The log gives their number. In Biomphalaria glabrata, 7 elements of 1.8-3.1 kb with hAT ends (14 bp TIRs, 8 bp TSD) were called LINE (I, RTE) on a reverse transcriptase alone and rejected.
- Solo LTRs of non-autonomous LTR elements: a second partner pass uses the `-NA` LTR elements of the first pass that have terminal LTRs of their own as partners for solo-LTR calls only (same `na_cfg$ltr_ident` and `na_cfg$solo_max`), among the classifier's Unknowns not called by the first pass or called `NA_low`. They take the partner's class and are rejected ("solo LTR of <-NA element>"); the log gives their number. In Biomphalaria glabrata, Gypsy-NA elements with 723-980 bp LTRs share only the LTR edges of their Gypsy partners (termini identity 0.25-0.37), and 18 elements of 688-980 bp, 86-98% identical to their LTRs, were called `NA_low` (14 accepted).
- Stats file: new column `homology_ends` (after `homology_notes`), and `TSD_superfamily` (after `TSD_target_tandem`): for DNA elements, the superfamily the TSD alone suggests, `DNA/TcMar` (TA), `DNA/PIF-Harbinger|DNA/PIF-ISL2EU` (AT), `DNA/hAT` (8 bp) or `DNA/PiggyBac|DNA/Kolobok` (TTAA); empty for other TSDs and for targets in short tandem repeats. The class is not changed. Over 737 DNA elements with a superfamily call in 14 genome sets, these TSDs named it as follows: TA 61 of 67, AT 23 of 23 (PIF), 8 bp 203 of 222 (3 of 7 in D. melanogaster, where P elements also have 8 bp TSDs), TTAA 46 of 48; other lengths are shared by several superfamilies.
- Results, same masked alignments as 1c83fb9 with `-t` (accepted elements): 1986 -> 2068 over 14 genome sets. 74 DNA elements accepted on their superfamily call (Ostrea edulis 34, Ephydatia muelleri 18, Biomphalaria glabrata 14, Nematostella vectensis 5, 3 more sets 1 each), 10 `NA_low` elements accepted, 7 elements called by the ends rule (all in B. glabrata), 9 solo LTRs of Gypsy-NA elements rejected (B. glabrata); no other element changes `pass` or class. Test data: 15 accepted, only the two new stats columns change. D. melanogaster and HG002: no change; E. cirrhosa 115 -> 117 (two Poseidon-NA elements, `NA_low`).

### Changed (microsatellites)
- Elements with no homology call that the classifier flags both `tandem` (TRF arrays cover >= 50%) and `low_complexity` (dustmasker covers >= 30%) are microsatellite arrays: class `Simple_repeat`, rejected (reason "microsatellite"). RepeatMasker masks simple repeats itself. Results, same alignments as 016e067: test and D. melanogaster unchanged (stats identical); HG002: 2 rejected Unknowns (79 and 95 bp, (AT)n and (CTTT)n) become Simple_repeat, accepted 18 -> 18; E. cirrhosa: accepted 169 -> 129, 42 Simple_repeat: 13 (TA)n of 51-334 bp accepted as DNA by their "TIRs" (the self-complementary repeat), 27 accepted as "Unknown: large cluster" ((TA)n, (CA)n, (AG)n, (AAG)n...), 2 already rejected. The `AGACATAG` family elements, flagged `tandem` but not `low_complexity`, are kept.

### Changed (tails, SINEs and contained elements)
- Tails: the 3' tail detector (`detect_tail`: polyA or a short tandem repeat, unit 1-6 bp, ending within 10 bp of an end) replaces the old polyA test (a run of `-p` A or T anywhere within 10 bp of either end) in the rules, and its minimum length is now `-p` (default 10; was fixed at 12). Stats column `polyA` is now TRUE when the tail at either end is an A tail.
- LINEs need a long ORF and a tail at the 3' end, or no terminal repeat, or a self-match away from both ends (`rules_cfg$line_max_pa` removed). With the orientation by protein call, the 3' end of a LINE is its right end.
- 3'-anchored relatives (`tr3_relatives`, `fragment_of`) are measured from the start of the container's 3' tail, not from its last base: copies of one family differ in polyA length.
- SINEs come only from the classifier's SINE models: the rule turning short Unknowns with a polyA near an end into SINEs is removed (`rules_cfg$sine_reco_*`). A tail alone over-calls: tried with a tail at either end, it made SINEs of 29 E. cirrhosa microsatellite arrays of 32-158 bp ((TA)n, (CA)n, (GAA)n...) and of human L1 3' ends. SINEs not oriented by a protein call that have a tail only at the 5' end are turned so that it is 3' (`orientation` `tail`).
- Contained elements take the class of their container (they kept their own, usually Unknown) and are rejected, also when no rule had accepted them ("no rule accepts it" becomes "contained in"); elements rejected for another reason (Ns, copies, tandem) keep their reason and take the container's class. An element that was Unknown at the homology step and is a 3' fragment of an accepted LINE or PLE (`fragment_of`: 90% of it at 90% identity, ending at the 3' end) counts as contained too: human L1 3' ends below the 98% containment identity were accepted as "Unknown: large cluster". The -e rule and containment now run before the final names are given, so the names carry the new class.
- Results, same alignments as 6351c23 (accepted; SINE; contained): test 16 -> 15 (SINE_1, 366 bp with a polyA and no SINE-model call, is now Unknown, rejected); D. melanogaster 101 -> 101; SINE 5 -> 0 (Jockey and BS 3' ends, already rejected, now LINE/I-Jockey); contained 29 -> 46 (17 Jockey fragments that no rule accepted). HG002 22 -> 18: 4 accepted L1 3' ends (3 Unknown, 1 SINE; Dfam L1PA/L1PA13 3end) are contained in L1_2 and take LINE/L1; 8 contained Unknown/SINE become LINE/L1; one L1PA4 3' end stays accepted as Unknown because its closest LINE is itself contained. E. cirrhosa 169 -> 169; contained 75 -> 112; 27 contained Unknowns take their container's class (L1-Tx1, RTE, Dong-R4, CR1, LTR, Poseidon). 3 accepted Unknowns that are 3' fragments of RTE_46 stay accepted, because RTE_46 itself is not accepted.

### Changed (orientation)
- Elements with a protein call are oriented by the strand of the protein behind the call, not by their longest ORF (stats: new column `orientation`, `protein`). The classifier writes that strand to `prot_calls.tsv` (new column `strand`: the strand of the protein, ORF or miniprot translation, with the highest score for the called label; `classification.tsv` is unchanged). In 999 VGP consensi of aPleWal, the longest ATG-to-stop ORF was on the other strand from the protein for 19 of 758 protein calls (14 PIF-Harbinger of 13.5-14.8 kb whose 340-aa transposase is shorter than an ORF on the other strand; an RTE whose 528-aa RT is not in its longest ORF).
- Elements without a protein call that are 3' fragments of a longer element oriented by its protein call (one BLAST hit covering at least 90% of the element at 90% identity or more, ending within 30 bp of the longer element's 3' end, as for `fragment_of`) are turned to the longer element's strand (`orientation` `container`). The others, including fragments already on their container's strand, keep the longest-ORF orientation (`ORF`). Solo LTRs and 5'-truncated LINE copies have short ORFs, so the longest ORF oriented them at random.
- Results, same alignments as 630f677: no element changes class, `pass` or `reason`; library sequences of the elements turned are reverse-complemented, with their alignments, and their tail, gap and 3'-relative columns change accordingly. Test data: unchanged (stats gain the column). D. melanogaster: 1 element oriented by protein on the other strand from its longest ORF (a Jockey fragment, FW), 7 turned by container; elements reversed relative to RepBase 8 -> 0 (6 solo LTRs, the Jockey fragment, a Jockey 3' end called SINE). Human HG002: 4 L1 3' ends (3 called SINE, 1 Unknown; Dfam L1HS/L1PA 3end) turned, their polyA now at the 3' end. E. cirrhosa: 14 Unknowns turned (3' ends of RTE, L1-Tx1, Dong-R4 and Poseidon elements); the 6 RTE ones now have their ATG tandem tail at the 3' end. Without the requirement that the container be oriented by protein, an AluY of HG002 was turned by a longer Alu consensus oriented by its longest ORF.

### Changed (element edges, TSDs and partners)
- Element edges come from svfind: each copy's svfind segment is the insertion without its TSD, found independently of the other copies, so the edges are the alignment columns where most copies' segments start and end. They are moved by at most 1 column each (`edges_cfg$refine_range`), to where the copies agree best on an exact TSD (up to 13 bp, `edges_cfg$refine_tsd_max`), trying every column tied for most copies. This replaces the edges from conservation moved by the TSD search, which moved edges into TIRs when TSD and terminal bases looked like a longer TSD at 80% identity (POGO: TA+CAGTA against TACTG+TA), and extended edges into target sites shared by the copies (ATAT-rich targets). Against RepBase 31.10 in five D. melanogaster genomes, consensus ends within 1 bp of RepBase: DNA 5' 8 -> 23 of 23, 3' 11 -> 22 of 23; LTR 5' 53 -> 73 of 82, 3' 49 -> 70 of 82; LINE 5' 4 -> 4, 3' 28 -> 32; both ends exact 29 -> 68 elements. Against Dfam in human HG002: 5' within 1 bp 3 of 17 -> 12 of 19, 3' 10 of 35 -> 15 of 29. The conservation edges and the TSD search are still used when no copy has svfind boundaries. A per-end fallback to the conservation edge where few copies agree on the svfind boundary is in the code but off (`edges_cfg$min_end_agree = 0`): at 0.3 it contained 3 more human L1 3' fragments with ragged ends, but D. melanogaster ends were a little worse and one Alu consensus was lost.
- svfind's empty-site overlap is kept (`parseONEview`: a B line with b_start > b_end means the two flanks of the empty allele overlap; the overlap is the target duplicated in the insertion allele), and written to `all_segments_flanks.tsv` (column `ov`).
- The minimum size (`-s`, and `loop2_cfg$min_cons_len`) is tested on the conserved block before the edges are trimmed, as svfind's segments were (a 98 bp TcMar MITE of the test data, 103 bp with the old edges, was lost). The segmental-duplication test uses the conservation edges too.
- TSD: maximum length 20 bp (was 13; `tsd_cfg$max_len`), for the 13-20 bp TSDs of target-primed reverse transcription (human Alu, L1, SVA). The TSD is decided by `tsd_svfind_rule`: if at least 2 copies have an svfind TSD (an empty-site overlap of at least 2 bp, `tsd_cfg$min_overlap`, confirmed by the copy's own flanks at 80% identity), the element has a TSD, of the flank caller's length if that is within the range of the copies' svfind TSDs, otherwise of their median length (source `svfind`). Without svfind support, the flank caller's TSD must beat a cross-copy null (the match rate of left and right flanks of different copies, which share target composition and motifs but no duplication; source `null`). The binomial test against base composition alone called a TSD in a third of D. melanogaster elements and two thirds of E. cirrhosa ones when each left flank was paired with another copy's right flank. TSD length in the known range of the superfamily (D. melanogaster, 103 elements, RepBase TSD lengths): 43 -> 92 (TcMar 9/11, hAT 3/4, P 4/4, CMC-Transib 2/2, MULE 2/2, Copia 4/4, Gypsy 49/54, Pao 19/22). HG002: Alu 13-17 bp, SVA 11-17, L1 0-19, LTR5_Hs/MER11C 5-6. In the test data, TcMar-Tc1_1 (3 copies) loses its TA (no svfind support, TA does not beat the null), Gypsy_1 and Pao_2 lose 2-3 bp TSDs outside their superfamily's range, TcMar-Tc1_2 goes from ATA to TA.
- TSD target in a short tandem repeat: the fraction of copies whose 20 flank bases on both sides repeat with a period of up to 6 bp (`tsd_cfg$tandem_*`). From half of the copies, the TSD is not used by the TSD-based reclassification rules (`Unknown to LTR`): in such targets any flank pair matches over many bases, so the TSD length is the length of the match in the repeat (E. cirrhosa elements in (TA)n reached the maximum length). Flagged: D. melanogaster 0 of 171 elements, HG002 1 of 43, E. cirrhosa 195 of 380.
- Containment ignores the tails of the contained element: its terminal tails (polyA or short tandem repeats, either end) are left out of the length to cover, and elements with a tail are also searched with the tail trimmed off, so that tail mismatches do not lower the identity (human L1 3' fragments with polyA tails longer than the container's, or mismatched, escaped containment and were accepted as large Unknown clusters).
- Partners by shared termini (`na_partner_calls`, `na_cfg`): an Unknown element whose two termini are those of an element classified by homology in the same run takes the partner's class. With a DNA, LTR or PLE partner, it is a non-autonomous derivative, `-NA` added to the class (e.g. `LTR/Gypsy-NA`), accepted, if it has the partner's edges (10 of the first and last 12 bases; either terminus may start up to 3 bases further in if the bases skipped are A, T or N, since consensi of elements in AT-rich microsatellite targets end a few bases into them) and the partner's terminal repeat (TIR, LTR, or 60 bp without one) at 90% identity or more (LTR: the whole LTR at both ends, 85%); otherwise the same class, as a low-confidence call (`partner_type` `NA_low`), accepted or rejected by the other rules. An Unknown no longer than 1.2 times the partner's LTR that matches it is a solo LTR, and an Unknown sharing both termini with a longer LINE an internal deletion: both take the partner's class and are rejected (the partner covers them). D. melanogaster: 2 BURDOCK internal deletions (2.3-2.4 kb, both LTRs) are `LTR/Gypsy-NA`, 9 solo LTRs and 3 Jockey/G6 internal deletions, all right by RepBase. E. cirrhosa: 8 Unknowns (134-696 bp) sharing both termini with Poseidon PLEs are `PLE/Poseidon-NA`: 3 (501-643 bp, 93% over the terminal 60 bp) `NA_high` and accepted (they were rejected as "LTR structure, mostly tandem repeat"), 5 `NA_low` (2 with a different 3' edge, 3 short ones, 134-276 bp, at 49-62%). HG002: none.
- Stats file: new columns `TSD_svfind` (most frequent empty-site overlap of the copies, bp), `TSD_svfind_support` (fraction of copies with it), `TSD_source` (`flanks`, `svfind`, `null`, `none`), `TSD_target_tandem` (fraction of copies in a short tandem repeat), `partner`, `partner_type` (`NA_high`, `NA_low`, `solo_LTR`, `internal_deletion`) and `partner_ident` (identity over the partner's terminal repeat, the lower of the two termini). The consensus names in intermediate files carry the TSD fields as `_tsdv`, `_tsdf`, `_tsds`, `_tsdt`.
- Results (accepted elements, baseline `94f0d72` -> this version, same alignments): test 16 -> 16 (Gypsy_1 accepted with terminal LTRs; SINE_2, 3 copies with ragged polyT, now has more Ns than allowed and is rejected as Unknown); D. melanogaster 98 -> 101 of 168 -> 171 (7 accepted with their structure: 5 Gypsy and 1 Pao with terminal LTRs, 1 Jockey; a truncated DOC, a BS 3' fragment called SINE, a G6 internal deletion and a BEL-5 solo LTR rejected, right by RepBase; accepted with the right class 94 -> 99); HG002 22 -> 22 (L1 3' fragments, all by Dfam: 2 called SINE are now contained in an L1, 3 that were contained now have longer, ragged svfind ends and are accepted as large Unknown clusters, and one called SINE is now Unknown); E. cirrhosa 236 -> 169 of 411 -> 380 (consensi no longer include the flanking (TA)n: both ends in (TA)n 92 -> 14 elements; 55 elements called DNA by artefactual TIRs, 50 of them with (TA)n at both ends, are Unknown; an LTR with a 7 bp svfind TSD is no longer reclassified; 3 non-autonomous Poseidon derivatives accepted).

### Changed
- Output: `<lib>-pantera-final.fa` is now the library of accepted elements only, and the rejected ones are written to `<lib>-pantera-rejected.fa`. `<lib>-pantera-final-pass.fa` is no longer written (its content is now `-final.fa`). The stats file still lists every element, accepted or rejected. The genome annotation (`annotations/`) uses only the accepted elements. Element names and numbering are unchanged.
- `scripts/compare_runs.sh` compares the accepted libraries, for runs of this and earlier versions.

### Added
- `scripts/pantera_classify.R`: the HMM classifier of TE sequences (protein domain HMMs, TIR and Helitron end models, SINE models, end partners, and flags for tandem, low-complexity, ncRNA and host-protein sequences), an R/data.table port of the unified caller of the panteraGA HMM classifier project (step 12). Used by panteraGA for the classification (see "Changed (classification)"); it also runs alone (`Rscript scripts/pantera_classify.R -i library.fa -o out -L pantera_hmm_v1 -T 8`) and writes `classification.tsv`, one row per sequence. Its output is identical, byte for byte, to that of the Python caller on the 48 test sequences, the VGP library aPleWal (999 consensi), 1,200 decoys and 12 more libraries. Results do not depend on the number of threads or the chunk size. The libraries (`pantera_hmm_v1.tar.gz`, 47 MB; optional host-protein part `pantera_hmm_v1_host.tar.gz`, 60 MB) are not in the repository. `scripts/test_classifier.sh <library folder>` checks the exact expected output on `test/hmm/` (input and expected classification). It needs HMMER, miniprot, Infernal, TRF and BLAST (and DIAMOND for the host-protein flag) on the `PATH`; they are not yet in the recipe.
- Tandem masking (`-t/--tandem_mask`, now the default, see above): the tandem arrays of each genome, found with FasTAN, are soft masked before aligning (FastGA `-M`), so FastGA starts no alignment in satellites and centromeres. Only arrays of at least `-w/--tandem_min_len` bp (default 1000; 0 masks all) are masked: short arrays add little alignment time, but are found inside some TEs, and masking them lost a young Gypsy with tandem repeats in its LTRs. With the two haplotypes of gorilla chromosome 22 (T2T), the alignment took 21 s instead of 632 s, masking 363 arrays of 1 kb or more (95% of the arrays' length); masking all 8,952 arrays took 20 s but kept fewer of the segments found without masking (176 of 201, against 186 masking arrays of 2 kb or more). Coordinates and sequences are those of the original genomes. The genome databases (`.1gdb`), FasTAN's arrays (`_tan.1ano`) and the masks (`_tan<w>.1ano`) are written to `tandem/` in the output folder, and kept with `-k` (the alignments refer to them) or `-d`. Needs FasTAN (new dependency, bioconda `fastan`); FAtoGDB and GIXmake come with FastGA.
- Stats file: column `reason`, after `pass`: the rule that accepted or rejected the element (the last rule that decided, since rules run in order and later ones overwrite earlier ones).
- Stats file: column `TSD_pvalue`, after `TSD_motif`: the p-value of the reported TSD (agreement between copies beyond chance, corrected for the TSD lengths tried). Empty when no TSD is reported. The consensus names in intermediate files carry it as `_tsdp`.

### Changed (rules)
- Contained elements are rejected as the last step, so no rule can accept them again: an element covered over at least 98% of its length, at 98% identity or more, by one hit of a longer accepted LINE, LTR, PLE or RC element (not DNA). The reason names the container. This replaces the previous step, which applied only to elements contained in LINEs (at more than 98%) and could be overridden by later rules.
- LINEs that are mostly a repeat of themselves (longest self-match, `maxTR`, over 40% of their length) are rejected, as LTR-structure elements already were. This replaces the rule that accepted every LINE with `maxTR` over 50 bp, after all other filters (including Ns and containment).
- Removed: the rule accepting any element with TIRs within 3 bp of both ends, whatever its class (DNA and Unknown elements with TIRs are accepted by their own rules), and the rule making Unknowns with TIRs and an 8 bp TSD DNA (the next rule made every Unknown with TIRs DNA anyway).

### Fixed
- `scripts/pantera_classify.R` no longer prints the harmless R warnings "no non-missing arguments to max/min" (for sequences whose protein hits have no lineage, and chunks with no protein hit left after filtering). The classification is unchanged.
- `scripts/test_classifier.sh` first checks that the tools are on the `PATH` (and names the missing ones) and prints which `Rscript` it uses. When the classifier fails, it prints the last 20 lines of its log, the exit status, the command and the path of the full log, which is kept.
- FastGA, GIXmake and FasTAN are run up to 3 times when they fail. FastGA's index step (GIXmake) sometimes crashes with a segmentation fault (seen with bioconda's FastGA 1.5.20260729, about once in a few runs of the test genomes) and stopped the whole run; run again, it works. Before a new attempt, the files left by the failed one are removed: the partial alignment, and the GDB, mask and index files FastGA had made next to the genomes (files that were there before the run are kept). Each retry is logged.
- `-t` with no tandem array at least `-w` bp long in a genome: GIXmake could not read the empty mask and the run stopped. That genome is now indexed without a mask.
- `-d/--debug` kept almost no intermediate files: the segments (`pantera_lib_0.fa`), the loop 1 output (`segments_candidates.fa`), `loop_1/`, `loop_2/` and the consensi were deleted at the end of every run. They are now kept with `-d`.
- `-i/--identity1` and `-y/--identity2` must be between 0.8 and 1. cd-hit-est refuses lower identities with the word length panteraGA uses, and the run stopped later with a misleading error ("cannot open the connection"). Values outside the range now stop the run at the start, with a message naming the option.
- A TSD length found in a single copy no longer counts as a TSD. A long chance match in one copy (e.g. 11 bp in 1 of 5 copies) passed the significance test, and its length was reported with confidence 0 and no motif. The minimum number of copies sharing a TSD length is now 2 (`tsd_cfg$min_support`, also used for the motif). In the test data, two SINEs lose such 10-11 bp "TSDs"; nothing else changes.

## [1.4.1]

Libraries, stats, alignments and annotations are identical to 1.4.0, and runs are faster: in a two-genome test (Eledone cirrhosa, 8 threads), processing after the FastGA alignments took about 30% less time. Two rare cases that stopped the run with an error now complete (see Fixed). The code is reorganised internally.

### Fixed
- No more R warnings "'/etc/localtime' is not a symlink" / "is not identical to any known timezone file" when running in a container: if `TZ` is unset and `/etc/localtime` is a regular file, panteraGA uses it directly (`TZ=:/etc/localtime`), keeping the host's local time.
- Loop 2 no longer stops the run when a cluster's alignment has no conserved block, or when a cluster holds a single sequence (possible only with `-m 1`): the cluster gives no consensus. Both cases used an R `next` outside a loop and would have stopped the run with an error; neither happened in the test runs.
- A file named `all_segments.fa` in the directory the run was started from (not the output folder) made the run stop with an error.
- The log overcounted removed duplicates ("Removed dups"): an element duplicated in several pairs was counted once per pair. Only the logged number was wrong.
- Log typos: "Processing N alignments" (was "Procesing"), "successfully". `scripts/compare_runs.sh` accepts both spellings.

### Changed
- The FastGA alignments are named after the two genomes they align: `<genome1>_<genome2>.1aln` (file names without folders or extensions), instead of `pantera<n>.1aln`. Alignments named `pantera<n>.1aln` by earlier versions are still reused (renamed) when found in the output folder. Genome files whose names are the same without folders and extensions (e.g. `a.fa.gz` and `a.fna`) are now refused.
- Internal thresholds that are not command-line options (loop 1 and 2 windows, consensus, edge and TSD search, classification probability, structure rules) are grouped in named settings near the top of the script, each with a short description. Results are unchanged.
- Loop 2's per-cluster work (`process_cluster_2`, about 450 lines) is split into separate functions: alignment, column consensus, raw edges, the TSD caller, the edge search, the TSD offset refinement and writing the alignment. Results are unchanged.
- panteraGA no longer changes the working directory (`setwd`): every file is written with its full path in the output folder, and file paths passed to external tools are quoted. Functions no longer assign global variables (`<<-`): the genome names and the segment flank lengths are set once in the main part of the script. Results are unchanged.
- The terminal TIR test is about 8 times faster: the 100 shuffled copies it compares each element with are aligned all at once, and only their scores are computed. Results are unchanged (same shuffles, same scores, same p-values).
- BLAST (classification, structure checks, annotation) splits the work between threads by query (`-mt_mode 1`). By default BLAST splits by database, and with one small database most threads stayed idle (annotation used 1.35 of 8 threads). Same hits, in the same order. Requires BLAST+ 2.12 or later (recipe updated).

### Removed
- Unused function `cdhit1` and commented-out code.

## [1.4.0]

### Added
- Checks before aligning: every genome in the list must exist and appear only once. The run stops if FastGA produces no alignment for a pair.
- With `-d`, loop 2 writes timing files per window and per alignment (`loop2_window_timing.tsv`, `loop2_mafft_timing.tsv`); `scripts/loop2_timing_summary.R` summarises them.
- Option `-I/--align_iterations`: maximum mafft refinement iterations for small clusters (< 100 sequences, < 3 kb), which mafft `--auto` aligns with L-INS-i and up to 1000 iterations. The default (1000) keeps those alignments unchanged. `-I 2` is somewhat faster (about 10% of the processing time in a test with ~600 elements) but changes some consensi slightly, and a few short elements are gained or lost.
- `-r/--seed` (default 1): runs are reproducible. The TIR test compares each candidate with shuffled copies, and without a fixed seed borderline TIRs could be detected in one run and not in the next. Each element is tested with random draws seeded from its own sequence and `--seed`, so the same data and seed give identical results, whatever `-T`, and an element's result does not depend on the other elements.

### Changed
- The run stops with an error when any parallel task fails (an R error, or a worker that ends without a result, for example killed for using too much memory), naming the step and the first failing window or cluster. Failed tasks used to be skipped silently, losing their segments or clusters.
- Loop 2 clusters each length window, then aligns all clusters as separate parallel tasks, largest first. Previously each window aligned its own clusters one after another, which left a few windows running alone at the end. Same results; loop 2 took about 40% less time on 8 genomes with 8 threads, and now scales with `-T`.
- mafft is called directly instead of through the R package ips, which is no longer a dependency (it brought 38 other R packages). Alignments are identical.
- In the alignment files (`alignments/*.maf`), each copy shows its flanks in lowercase and the insertion itself in uppercase. The library and the stats are not affected.

### Fixed
- Cluster members could be silently lost when reading cd-hit's cluster files, if the lines R sampled to guess the file's columns held only single-sequence clusters (shown as the R warning "Stopped early ... Expected 3 fields but found 4"). Clusters could then fall below `-m` and be discarded. The files are now read line by line. This affected all earlier versions.
- `-s/--min_size` and `-l/--max_size` now apply to the insertion itself. They were applied to the insertion plus its 200 bp of flanks, so `-s 100` let through insertions from 50 bp (the minimum passed to svfind). The default `-s` is now 50, which keeps the same segments as before; values below 50 are also passed to svfind. Consensus sequences must still be at least 100 bp long (or `-s`, if larger), as before.
- The flanks removed before clustering are the exact ones reported by svfind (its `X` line) instead of a fixed 100 bp per side, which also left 1 flank base on each side.
- `-n/--max_ns` had no effect: Ns were counted in uppercase on sequences that are still lowercase at that point. (With current FastGA versions segments contain no Ns, so results do not change.)
- The "Largest/Smallest insertion" log lines report the insertion length (previously "segment", with the round 2 values inconsistent).

### Packaging

- `-h` exits with status 0; errors exit with status 1 and are reported on stderr.
- Portable shell redirection (`>/dev/null 2>&1`).

### Fixed
- `-y/--identity2` was ignored (round 2 clustered at `-i`).
- `-p` (polyA length) was ignored.
- `-n` is now a double (was declared integer).
- `-T` now also limits BLAST; mafft runs single-threaded inside the parallel workers.
- The flanking filter was computed but never applied; `-f` other than 100 crashed.
- LTR reclassification used the TIR table.
- Pass logic is now explicitly "fail unless rescued".
- Crashes on edge cases: no ORF hits, no self-BLAST hits, empty svfind output, `kneedle` failures, clusters without a conserved block.
- Per-consensus data (cluster size, TSD, genome counts) could be swapped between elements of equal length.
- Element names now match the final class (`<class>_<n>-<lib>#<class>`), numbered by length within class; alignment files renamed to match and reverse-complemented when the consensus is flipped (`_R_` prefix toggled).
- The null base composition for TSD statistics was always 0.25; it is now computed from the flanks.

### Changed
- Flanking filter: discards the fraction `-q` of segments with the shortest flank (the previous rule removed ~95% of segments).
- Consensus ties: transition ties (A/G, C/T) resolve to A/C; other ties become N. Consensus threshold 0.3.
- Edge search: inward shifts cannot exceed the TSD length.
- TSD support is tested for significance (binomial, corrected for the lengths and edge positions tried).
- Edge/TSD search rewritten (13–20x faster, identical results).
- Terminal TIR finder (anchored local alignment with a shuffled null, p <= 0.01) replaces the `short_tir` heuristic.
- Unknown elements with TG...CA termini and a 4–6 bp TSD (confidence > 0.8) are reclassified as LTR.
- DNA elements with a TIR within 8 bp of both ends pass.
- LINEs pass with a 3' tail (polyA or short tandem repeat) or 3'-anchored relatives.

### Added
- Options `-q/--flank_quantile` (default 0.05) and `-c/--max_cons_ns` (default 0.02).
- Stats columns `homology_class`, `homology_prob`, `tail3_motif`, `tail3_len`, `tail5_motif`, `tail5_len`, `tr3_relatives`, `fragment_of`.
